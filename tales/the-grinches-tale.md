# The Grinches Tale

*Chosen for the Whobilation reading by Cindy-Lou Who, who is small and
picks long ones. Copied out as it was found, spelling and all.*

---

Heere bigynneth the Grinches Tale.

Upon a mountayne, fer above the Whos,
Ther dwelte a wight that hadde no love to lose;
Ful grene his hyde, his thoughtes derk and blake;
Upon his bak he heng an empty sak.
Doun from his cave he crepte at derke nyght,
Arrayed as Seint Nicholas, from on heigh;
He stal hir tree, hir tynsel, and, for sothe,
Hir roost and mele, and laughed, and swoor his ooth:
“No song shal ryse on Cristemasse morwe, lo!
Ne bel shal rynge, ne Who be glad, nat so!”
Daun Pardoner wolde seyn it, doutelees,
Of alle synnes, coveitise is nat leest.

Heere endeth the Grinches Tale.

---

*A note from the School:* the last couplet is the Pardoner's line, and
the Pardoner preached always upon the one text and no other. Miss Rue
says any Who who has sat in her class can give it back in the Latin
without looking it up. She says it is the sort of thing a pilgrim learns
at the inn and never forgets.
